# Expert Review: "Spectral Integrity in Lightweight Multivariate Time Series Forecasting"

> **Reviewer stance:** PhD-level expert in time series forecasting. This review is structured as a formal paper critique — identifying factual inconsistencies between the paper's claims and the underlying code/results, methodological weaknesses, and missing components required for a credible publication.

---

## 0. Overall Assessment

The paper identifies a genuinely interesting diagnostic question — *do time-domain metrics hide subband failures?* — and builds a reasonable experimental apparatus around it. However, as currently written it suffers from several **hard factual inconsistencies** between the LaTeX manuscript and the actual results files, significant **methodological gaps** that undermine the strength of the claims, and a **scope problem**: the multi-dataset SOTA table (Table 1) compares apples to oranges, since only ETTh1 was actually trained in this work.

---

## 1. Hard Factual / Verification Issues

### 1.1 ❌ The "up to 9.3% mid-band reduction" claim is not reproducible from the results

The abstract and Table 2 caption both state:
> *"dual-domain projections reduce mid-band error by up to **9.3%**"*

**Actual computation from `spectral_benchmark_summary.csv`:**

| Horizon | DLinear mid-MSE | Best hybrid mid-MSE | Reduction |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|-----------|
| H=96    | 0.068186       | 0.064862 (wavelet)  | **4.87%** |
| H=192   | 0.067005       | 0.063743 (wavelet)  | **4.87%** |
| H=336   | 0.071060       | 0.067669 (wavelet)  | **4.77%** |
| H=720   | 0.074616       | 0.071018 (freq)     | **4.82%** |

The maximum reduction across all horizons is **~4.9%**, not 9.3%. There is **no configuration in the results files that produces a 9.3% improvement**. This is a significant factual error in both the abstract and the caption of Table 2.

> [!CAUTION]
> This is the most damaging issue. A reviewer running basic arithmetic on Table 2 will catch this immediately.

---

### 1.2 ❌ Multi-dataset SOTA Table (Table 1) uses externally sourced numbers for datasets not trained in this work

The paper claims:
> *"we construct Table 1... evaluating our dual-domain lightweight models against... PatchTST, HDMixer, CycleNet, DLinear"*

**What is in the results folder:** Only `ETTh1` JSON files exist. There are **no result files** for ETTh2, ETTm1, Weather, or Traffic — the four other datasets in Table 1.

**The caption says:**
> *"SOTA baseline metrics for HDMixer, PatchTST, and DLinear are reproduced directly from the canonical AAAI-24 HDMixer benchmark evaluation protocol"*

But the **"Ours" column numbers** for ETTh2, ETTm1, Weather, and Traffic cannot come from this work's experiments. They are either fabricated, interpolated, or copied from another source — **this is not disclosed anywhere in the paper**.

> [!CAUTION]
> If the "Ours" numbers for non-ETTh1 datasets are reproduced from external sources without attribution, this borders on research misconduct. If they were generated by this codebase, the raw results must exist somewhere. Either way, this requires immediate clarification.

---

### 1.3 ❌ DLinear MSE in Table 1 does not match your own experimental results

The paper reports in Table 1 for ETTh1, H=96:
- DLinear MSE = **0.375**

But `dlinear_ETTh1_H96.json` shows:
- DLinear MSE = **0.4296**

This is a large discrepancy (~14%). The paper is apparently using the DLinear number from the HDMixer paper's table rather than from its own run. This is fine if acknowledged, but the paper also claims to compare **"our dual-domain models"** against these numbers — and the "Ours" entry at H=96 is 0.371 (MSE), while the actual experiment result for FreqEnhanced at H=96 is **0.4163** from the CSV.

**In short: the entire first table mixes two incompatible experimental protocols.** The baseline numbers come from the HDMixer paper (different hyperparameters, potentially different train/test splits, L=336 from a different paper's convention), while "Ours" numbers are presumably from this code which uses a 70/10/20 split — **not the canonical ETT split**.

> [!WARNING]
> The standard ETT split is: Train=8545, Val=2881, Test=2881 (for ETTh1). Your `experiment.py` uses a 70/10/20 ratio on the whole dataset, which gives a different test set. This means your numbers are not comparable to the SOTA literature numbers.

---

### 1.4 ⚠️ Epoch count is severely under-specified for benchmark competition

`experiment.py` defaults to `--epochs 5`. Standard practice for ETT benchmarks (DLinear, PatchTST, HDMixer) is **10–100 epochs with early stopping**. Five epochs is unlikely to converge, especially for the spectral and wavelet branches. The paper does not report the number of training epochs, which is a critical reproducibility omission.

---

### 1.5 ⚠️ The abstract claims $FVU_\text{low} \approx 0.51$ — this is only true at H=96

From the results:
- H=96: FVU_low = 0.510 ✓
- H=192: FVU_low = 0.547
- H=336: FVU_low = 0.605
- H=720: FVU_low = 0.713

The abstract presents 0.51 as a general characteristic of DLinear on ETTh1, but it degrades substantially. The phrase "reliably captures low-frequency trends" becomes increasingly misleading at longer horizons.

---

### 1.6 ⚠️ The spectral coherence metric at H=96–336 is 1.0 for all models — suspicious

From `spectral_benchmark_summary.csv`:

| H   | dlinear coherence | freq coherence | wavelet coherence |
|-----|-------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| 96  | 1.000             | 1.000          | 1.000             |
| 192 | 1.000             | 1.000          | 1.000             |
| 336 | 1.000             | 1.000          | 1.000             |
| 720 | 0.303             | 0.306          | 0.307             |

A coherence of 1.0 for H=96–336 across all models is physically implausible for a forecasting task — it would imply perfect phase alignment. This strongly suggests the `spectral_coherence_analysis` function in `metrics.py` has a bug, likely because `nperseg=min(256, seq_len)` equals `pred_len` (96, 192, 336 are all ≤ 256), causing the Welch estimator to use a single segment equal to the full signal, which can produce degenerate coherence estimates. **The coherence results are not mentioned in the paper, but they invalidate the `mean_coherence` metric entirely.**

---

### 1.7 ⚠️ High-band FVU > 1.0 for all models — this result is understated

The paper claims DLinear suffers from "over-smoothing on periodic mid-frequency dynamics" (FVU_mid ≥ 0.81). But the data also shows:

| H   | DLinear FVU_high | Freq FVU_high | Wavelet FVU_high |
|-----|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 96  | **1.112**       | 1.021         | 1.024           |
| 192 | **1.123**       | 1.020         | 1.031           |
| 336 | **1.131**       | 1.029         | 1.040           |
| 720 | **1.149**       | 1.037         | 1.054           |

FVU > 1.0 means the model performs **worse than predicting the mean** in the high-frequency band — i.e., it is actively injecting noise. Even the hybrid models fail here (FVU > 1.0). This is a significant finding that the paper **completely ignores**. A discussion of high-band failure is essential.

---

### 1.8 ⚠️ The energy ratio claim in the abstract is directionally inconsistent

The abstract says: *"energy ratio ≈ 0.31"* for mid-band. The actual value at H=96 is 0.318, which rounds to 0.32. More importantly, the hybrid models have **lower** energy ratios (freq: 0.287, wavelet: 0.280) — meaning they attenuate mid-band energy **more** than DLinear, yet have lower FVU. This counter-intuitive result deserves an explanation: is the model finding a more accurate but lower-amplitude periodic representation, or is it fitting noise differently? The paper does not address this.

## 2. Methodological Weaknesses

### 2.1 Single dataset for the core spectral analysis

The primary contribution (Parseval FVU analysis) is only demonstrated on **ETTh1**. ETTh1 is a well-known dataset with strong 24h diurnal cycles, which artificially amplifies the mid-band story. Without replication on:
- ETTm1 (15-min resolution, different periodicity structure)
- Weather (highly non-stationary)
- Traffic (heavy-tailed, irregular)

…the generalizability claim is not established.

### 2.2 No statistical significance testing

There are no confidence intervals, no standard deviation across random seeds, no statistical significance tests. The improvements between hybrid and DLinear in Table 2 are small (~4.9% at best). Without multiple seeds, there is no way to know if these differences are signal or noise.

### 2.3 Frequency band boundaries are fixed and unjustified

The code uses `low_cutoff=0.1`, `high_cutoff=0.4` hardcoded in `metrics.py`. The paper defines the boundaries symbolically ($\omega_\text{low}$, $\omega_\text{high}$) but never states their numerical values or justifies them. For ETTh1 at 1-hour resolution, the dominant diurnal cycle sits at normalized frequency 1/24 ≈ 0.042, which falls in the **low** band, not the mid band. This undermines the claim that the mid-band captures "diurnal cycles" and "weekly patterns."

> [!IMPORTANT]
> With `low_cutoff=0.1` and `high_cutoff=0.4`:
> - f=1/24 ≈ 0.042 → **low band** (diurnal cycle)  
> - f=1/168 ≈ 0.006 → **low band** (weekly cycle)  
> - The mid band [0.1, 0.4] covers cycles of length 2.5–10 time steps — these are **not** interpretable as "dominant quasi-periodic cycles (diurnal cycles, weekly patterns)" as stated in the paper.

This is a serious conceptual mismatch between the paper's narrative and its implementation.

### 2.4 The wavelet model architecture contradicts the paper description

**Paper (§3.3):** Applies DWT to the seasonal component $\mathbf{X}_s$ (after decomposition), then learns $\hat{cA}$ and $\hat{cD}$, reconstructs via IDWT, then applies a `seq_len → H` projection.

**`models.py` (`WaveletProjectionBranch`):** Applies DWT to the **raw input** `x`, not the seasonal component. Furthermore, it projects each coefficient array **within its own length** (not to `H`), reconstructs via IDWT back to `seq_len`, then applies a final linear `seq_len → pred_len`. This is a different architecture from what the paper describes.

> [!WARNING]
> The math in §3.3 says $\hat{cD} = W_D \cdot cD$ where $W_D : L/2 \to ?$. It never specifies the output dimension of the wavelet projection, and the final `W_proj: L → H` is missing from the equation. The code and paper are describing different models.

### 2.5 The Fourier model's cross-term coupling is missing

**Paper (§3.2):** Projects real and imaginary parts independently:
$$\hat{Z}_\text{real} = W_\text{real} \cdot \text{Re}(\text{rFFT}(X))$$
$$\hat{Z}_\text{imag} = W_\text{imag} \cdot \text{Im}(\text{rFFT}(X))$$

**`models.py` (`SpectralProjectionBranch`):** This matches the paper. However, the paper doesn't acknowledge that projecting real and imaginary parts independently with separate weight matrices is **not** equivalent to a general linear map in the complex domain (which would require cross-terms: $\hat{Z}_\text{real} = W_{rr} \cdot \text{Re}(X) - W_{ri} \cdot \text{Im}(X)$). This is a known limitation (essentially only learning amplitude, not phase rotation), but it is not discussed.

### 2.6 No ablation study

There is no ablation isolating the contribution of:
1. The spectral branch alone vs. gated fusion
2. The effect of α initialization at 0.0
3. The kernel size of the moving average in the decomposition (hardcoded at 25, not reported)
4. Different wavelet families (only Haar level=1 is tested, despite the paper claiming "DWT" generally)

### 2.7 Training split does not follow standard protocol

`experiment.py` splits data as 70%/10%/20% (train/val/test). The standard ETT benchmark protocol (used by DLinear, PatchTST, HDMixer, etc.) uses a **fixed chronological split** by timestamp, not percentage-based. This makes every number this paper produces incomparable to published results without re-running with the correct split.

### 2.8 No early stopping — training may not be converged

The code has no early stopping mechanism. With 5 epochs and no validation-based stopping, the reported results may reflect under-trained models, making any comparison to published SOTA meaningless.

## 3. Missing Content for a Complete Paper

### 3.1 Complexity analysis is absent

The paper claims the hybrid models maintain "edge-efficiency advantages" but there is no FLOPs or parameter count comparison. The wavelet branch adds a `WaveletProjectionBranch` with `levels × channels × coeff_length` extra linear layers. At `channels=7`, this is manageable, but the claim needs quantitative backing.

### 3.2 No visualization of frequency-domain predictions

A paper about spectral integrity has **no spectral plots** (no PSD plots, no spectrogram comparisons, no visualization of the FVU across the frequency axis). The architecture TikZ figure is the only figure in the paper. This is a major weakness for a frequency-domain paper.

### 3.3 The gating convergence claim needs a training curve

§4.3 reports that $\sigma(\alpha)$ converges near 50% and increases with horizon. This is reported as a single number with no training curve, no variance across seeds, and no discussion of whether the gate ever converges to a degenerate solution (all-time or all-frequency).

### 3.4 No comparison to FreTS, FiLM, or other pure frequency-domain baselines

The related work cites frequency-domain methods (FEDformer, FreqCycle, FreqLens, WaveMoE) but never compares against pure-frequency baselines like **FreTS** or **FiLM** (Frequency Improved Legendre Memory). This weakens the experimental section.

### 3.5 RevIN is mentioned but not used

The related work section mentions Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN) as an important normalization technique. The code does **not** use RevIN. The paper should either apply RevIN (as most SOTA methods do) or explicitly justify its omission.

### 3.6 References are incomplete / placeholder-quality

Several `.bib` entries are placeholder-quality:
- `wpmixer2024`: `author={Zhang, Y. and others}` — incomplete
- `WaveMoE2026`: `author={Wang, X. and others}` — incomplete
- `FreqCycle2026`, `FreqLens2026`: same pattern

These would be rejected by any venue's bibliographic standards.

---

## 4. Positive Aspects Worth Preserving

- The Parseval decomposition framework in §2.2–2.3 is **correctly implemented** in `metrics.py` (`fft_band_wise_error_decomposition`). The energy conservation identity holds by construction.
- The `is_low=True` DC-component correction in the FVU denominator is methodologically sound.
- The intuition that time-domain MSE optimization incentivizes predicting the conditional mean (and thus over-smoothing) is correct and well-motivated.
- The gating convergence result (~50%) is a legitimate finding, though it needs multiple seeds.
